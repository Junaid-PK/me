### **“Leading When You’re Not the Loudest in the Room”**

For a long time, I thought I couldn’t be a leader because I wasn’t the loudest voice in meetings.

I wasn’t the one whiteboarding aggressively. I wasn’t the person who always had a hot take ready. I didn’t have that natural gravitas you see in Hollywood portrayals of “strong leaders.”

So I stayed quiet. Not because I had nothing to say—but because I thought my style didn’t fit the mold.

What if leadership isn’t volume?

What if it’s presence? Consistency? The kind of reliability that makes people *want* to follow you—not just hear you?

When I was promoted to lead a new backend team, I worried they’d see me as passive. I wasn’t going to outtalk anyone. But I could outlisten.

And that made all the difference.

I made space in meetings for the voices that always got cut off. I asked questions that slowed things down just enough for real discussion. And when a conflict flared between two senior devs, I didn’t intervene loudly—I talked to each of them separately. Helped them understand each other.

It wasn’t flashy. But it worked.

If you're a quieter leader, lean into it:

* **Use silence as a tool.** Pauses create space. You don’t have to fill them to be effective.
* **Frame before you speak.** A well-placed question can redirect an entire room.
* **Model calm under pressure.** Quiet confidence is contagious.
* **Lead through presence.** Being consistently dependable is louder than any speech.

Leadership isn’t reserved for the loud. It’s available to the intentional, the thoughtful, the steady.

Your voice matters—even if it isn’t the one people hear first. Sometimes, it’s the one they remember longest.
